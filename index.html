About Intrinsa

Intrinsa estimates what a listed company may be worth, using several independent valuation methods rather than a single number. Type a company name or ticker symbol and the app returns a structured valuation report: a blended fair value, each method's contribution, a Monte Carlo range of outcomes, analyst context and charts.

Intrinsa is available for iPhone. It is built for investors, students and anyone who wants to understand how valuation works, not just see a price.

Getting started

Important: Intrinsa is a client for the Intrinsa valuation engine. Calculations run on a valuation server, and the app connects to it using a server address and API key entered in Settings. The app does not provide valuation data without a configured server.

1. Connect to your server

Open Settings in the app and enter your server address and API key. If you received access from us or your organisation, use the details you were given.

2. Run a valuation

  1. Open the Valuation tab.
  2. Type a company name or ticker (for example Apple or AAPL).
  3. Press Return to look it up and instantly show any result that has already been calculated.
  4. Tap Run Valuation to start a fresh calculation. This usually takes a few minutes.

3. Optional settings

  • Peer tickers: add comma-separated tickers to choose the comparison group yourself. Leave blank to let the engine pick peers.
  • News source: leave on auto unless you have a reason to change it.
  • Advanced options: adjust model assumptions if you know what you're changing.

4. Watchlists and reports

In Watchlist, enter one ticker per line, choose how many to run at once (concurrent workers) and tap Run Watchlist. Results are listed with the highest upside first. Every completed valuation is saved under Reports.

How the valuation works

MethodWhat it measures
Discounted cash flow (DCF)Present value of projected future free cash flows, discounted at the company's cost of capital (WACC).
ComparablesValue implied by peer multiples: P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/Revenue and P/B.
Dividend discount modelPresent value of expected future dividends, for dividend-paying companies.
Net asset valueBook assets minus liabilities, per share. Usually low for asset-light businesses.
Residual incomeBook value plus the present value of earnings above the cost of equity.
Monte CarloRuns the DCF many times with varied inputs to show a range of plausible outcomes.
Reverse DCFThe growth rate the current share price implies.

The blended fair value combines the applicable methods using weights shown in the report. Analyst targets and the market price are displayed for reference only and are not part of the blend. Upside or downside is the difference between the blended fair value and today's price.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the fair value so different from the share price?

Intrinsic value models are deliberately conservative and based on fundamentals such as cash flow and assets. Market prices also reflect sentiment, growth expectations and factors no model captures. A large gap is a prompt to look at the assumptions, not a verdict. Check the per-method values, the Monte Carlo range and the reverse DCF to see why.

Why is net asset value so low for some companies?

Companies such as software or technology firms hold most of their value in intangibles (brands, software, people) that don't appear on the balance sheet. That's why net asset value usually gets a small weight in the blend for these companies.

Why do the comparables show the same low and high value?

When a single peer multiple is used, the range collapses to one figure. Adding more peer tickers widens the comparison.

How long does a valuation take?

Usually a few minutes, because the engine downloads data, runs simulations and trains the forecast model. Pressing Return on a ticker shows any already-calculated result instantly.

How accurate is the next-day forecast?

It is a statistical estimate, shown with its RMSE (typical error in dollars) from validation. Short-term prices are noisy; treat the forecast as an illustration, not a prediction to trade on.

Which markets are supported?

Listed companies covered by the engine's data sources, including major US exchanges and many international listings such as Toronto. Private companies cannot be valued.

Where does the data come from?

Valuations use publicly available market and financial-statement data retrieved by the valuation server. Data can be delayed, incomplete or out of date.

Is my data stored?

Saved reports and your server settings are kept on your device. Tickers you look up are sent to the valuation server you configured. See the Privacy Policy for details.

Troubleshooting

  • "Company not found": check the spelling or try the ticker. Private or delisted companies can't be valued.
  • Several companies match: choose the correct listing and exchange from the list.
  • Can't connect to the server: check your internet connection, then confirm the server address and API key in Settings.
  • Valuation stuck or slow: runs can take several minutes. If one hasn't finished after 10 minutes, go back and run it again.
  • Some methods are missing: methods are skipped when data isn't available, for example the dividend model for companies that don't pay dividends.
  • Old results: check the "Updated" timestamp on the report and tap Run Valuation for fresh figures.
